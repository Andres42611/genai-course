# Part 3 — Architecture Decision Memo

Hi Product team,

I hope this message finds you well. In terms of the current project with retinal fundus images, I would like to propose we start with a VAE architecture to augment our dataset, primarily because of certain characteristics of our dataset and also key traits VAEs have that I think will be helpful.

Our dataset is relatively small, so when comparing it to GANs and diffusion models, that's the first thing we need to account for. GANs and diffusion models require much higher data thresholds, and on top of the fact that our data is uneven across five severity levels, I worry that starting there would produce results that are not helpful or meaningful. VAEs are efficient, stable, and more robust to dataset size and quirks, and I think they will fit well given our 40 A10G hour budget.

With only 40 hours and 800 images, diffusion models are too computationally greedy and not suited for our goal to augment rather than maximize realism. Even if highly realistic images become a concern, I think that visual quality would not justify this first round, where we first want to show that clinically meaningful features can improve downstream classification.

Similarly, GAN training is vulnerable to mode collapse, so I could see it overrepresenting dominant patterns and ultimately failing to represent rare levels reliably. Because the five severity levels are unevenly represented, mode collapse would mean generating too narrow a range of outputs. With our uneven dataset, this could disproportionately silence or exclude rare severity levels, which would work against our concerns about false negatives.

The primary failure mode we need to measure is oversmoothing and information loss. VAEs, although fast and lightweight in data needs, could cause subtle disease features to blur or disappear during the compression and reconstruction of images through latent space. FID, a commonly used metric in the field to measure statistical similarity between our generated images' distribution and the real data distribution, could tell us whether our synthetic images resemble the spread of the real images. However, it cannot tell us whether the specific features we need to identify retinal diseases were preserved: a retinal image that looks very realistic to the human eye could still lose a small lesion or pathological feature that changes how severe or early we catch the symptoms.

Thus, we cannot base failure-mode tracking on visual quality or FID alone. I propose we train and evaluate sensitivity (True Positive Rate) on the downstream classifier using (i) real images only and then (ii) real images plus synthetic data. If our synthetic data reduces disease sensitivity, that is a major warning to us. This metric would help us address our concern regarding false negatives and ensure that the VAE is keeping the key features needed to generate severity levels for our downstream classifier. We could apply this metric on a per-class level to ensure that less-represented classes are being caught and classified well, and ultimately have a strong downstream classifier with confidence that the data being used is capturing what we need at the end of the day.
